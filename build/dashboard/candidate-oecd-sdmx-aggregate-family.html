The question

what a model is asked to predict

The lead probed was the OECD's public statistical catalogue - the SDMX API behind the OECD Data Explorer, 1,550 dataflows measured - as a source of prediction tasks. The task family it supports is 'will indicator X for geographic unit G rise/fall in the next period' over country/city panels (annual, quarterly or monthly reference periods). The session probed the licence (class A, clean), measured the grain of the catalogue across three flagship families, and rejected the family on task shape: every sampled flow is a geographic-unit x period aggregate panel, which is persistence-dominated and mirrored by the originating national sources.

What you predict

target definition

Would have been a binary change target on a country/city x period indicator series (e.g. quarter-over-quarter rise in a short-term indicator), because a level target on a persistent series is the measured nhs-ae failure mode. The change target on annual country panels has few independent positives (one global recession year marks every country at once), which is why the family was rejected rather than reformulated.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

Would have been the end of reference period P (year/quarter/month) with features from periods <= P. The publisher does republish on schedule (monthly/quarterly/annual per flow, plus revision policies), so publication honesty would have been workable - the failure is the task shape, not the timing.
