tsb-railway-occurrence-casualty

published · CA · 2026-10-22 13:50 UTC

The question

what a model is asked to predict

A railway occurrence begins at a known place, time and operating context on the Canadian network (crossing accident, derailment, trespasser event, fire, collision, movement exceeding limits of authority, ...). At that instant, given only pre-occurrence context - where (subdivision, nearest location, province, region, lat/long), which railway, when (date, time of day, season), and what the train was doing (activity type) - will the occurrence result in at least one casualty (fatality or any injury)?

What you predict

target definition

Binary: occurrence resulted in >=1 casualty. Label source columns: InjuriesIND ('Injuries indicator'), cross-checked against TotalFatalInjuries / TotalSeriousInjuries / TotalMinorInjuries ('Total fatal/serious/minor injuries') and the separate INJURIES table (RODSdb_MDOTW_VW_INJURIES_PUBLIC.csv, 2,710,400 bytes). All are excluded from the shipped feature columns by construction.

When the prediction is made

the information cut-off

The instant the occurrence begins (OccDate/OccTime, with the documented local time zone via TimeZoneID). Everything the TSB records during or after the occurrence is excluded from the shipped features: TSB's accident/incident type assignment (its taxonomy embeds outcomes, e.g. 'NON-MAIN-TRACK TRAIN DERAILMENT (NO DAMAGE)'), all injury/DG-release/fire/evacuation/damage flags and counts, weather observations taken at the occurrence, track and rolling-stock attributes measured during the occurrence, investigation/deployment/closeout fields, process dates (ENTRY_DT, RELEASE_DT, DAILY_RELEASED_DT, OccClosedDate), the free-text Summary, and even ReportedDate/ReportedTime (the notification instant, which post-dates the onset) - the last is shipped only in the record's provenance, never as a feature. The casualty outcome unfolds after onset and is the label.
