We dedicate our contribution to the public domain under **CC0-1.0** (`LICENSE-CC0-1.0.txt`). That

**This dedication does not cover MSHA's data.** It is a statement about our own contribution.

## The source's status - public domain by statute, preserved

MSHA publishes the Violations, Inspections and Mines data sets with **no licence, no terms of use
and no redistribution permission** stated on the data-set pages or in the definition files; the
data.gov catalogue entry (`https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/msha-violations-dataset`) carries
`accessLevel: public` and `rights: "true"` and names no licence. The basis for redistribution is
therefore statutory, not a quotable grant: works of the US federal government are not subject to
copyright (**17 USC 105**). No publisher grant is claimed anywhere in this release.

Two independent third parties already redistribute derived MSHA data under open dedications
(emperor-mew/msha-violations, CC0; FastDOLz/msha-mine-safety-violations-by-operator, CC-BY-4.0,
Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20572372). That was weighed as precedent for the statutory reading, not
as permission.

The operator decision resolving this question (2026-10-12, option A: treat US federal works as
public domain for this source, record the reasoning, proceed without seeking written permission)
is recorded with its evidence in the factory repository. If MSHA ever publishes terms, this file
and that decision record are where the change lands.

**Therefore: this is a derived dataset and is not an official MSHA or US Department of Labor
product.** The derivation is labelled as such in the README, the data dictionary and the manifest.
